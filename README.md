# General idea
This project proposes an innovative agent based simulation to model the non-linear dynamics of customer churn by integrating social contagion theory with a microscopic cognitive architecture. At the interaction layer, the simulation employs the non-linear $q$-voter model, where agents consult a group of $q$ neighbors to determine their behavioral state, either conforming to a unanimous consensus or flipping their opinion with a probability $\epsilon$ when disagreement occurs — a parameter that effectively simulates the impact of external marketing noise and advertising intensity. This framework introduces an internal cognitive layer for each agent using a percolation matrix, which mimics the structural breakdown of brand loyalty or the formation of new neural pathways as a "burning tree" process. The internal flammability or connectivity probability within an agent's mental grid is driven by the weighted influence of external ads and social pressure, leading to a state transition only when a spanning cluster is formed.

# What will the model be used for?
The primary objective of this model is to serve as a strategic risk assessment tool that identifies the critical thresholds where marketing interventions lose efficiency against the momentum of social contagion. By simulating various scenarios, the model will calculate the exit probability and the average time required for a population to reach a consensus state of churn. This enables stakeholders to move beyond static churn metrics and instead conduct synthetic A/B testing on parameters like the number of influential neighbors consulted $q$ and the probability of state flipping due to external noise $\epsilon$. From a technical perspective, the model functions as a synthetic data generator designed to populate a structured database, where phase shifts from a disordered paramagnetic phase to an ordered ferromagnetic absorbing state asserting churn as a systemic event. Ultimately, the model will provide a quantitative bridge between microscopic interaction rules and macroscopic system health, offering actionable insights into whether a customer base is approaching a "point of no return" dictated by the underlying structural dynamics of the network.

# Why model this?
Modeling churn through this combined framework is essential because traditional predictive analytics often overlook the collective behavior and non-linear transitions inherent in social systems. By employing the $q$-voter model, we can observe how the system moves between a disordered paramagnetic phase and an ordered ferromagnetic phase depending on microscopic interaction rules and noise levels $\epsilon$. This approach allows the reveal of how external perturbations, such as marketing ads, act as noise that can either stabilize the system or push it past a critical threshold into a consensus state from which it cannot recover. From a technical standpoint, this simulation provides a rigorous testing ground for analyzing how finite-dimensional fluctuations in 2D networks renormalize deterministic potentials and wash out intermediate phases, a level of detail that standard churn models cannot provide. 

# Who are the agents?
The agents in this simulation are modeled as heterogeneous social actors, specifically customers within a brand ecosystem, who are characterized by a binary state variable representing either active engagement or churn. Architecturally, each agent is defined by two distinct layers: an external social interaction layer following the rules of the nonlinear q-voter model and an internal cognitive layer represented by a percolation matrix. On the macro scale, agents are embedded within a network — such as a 2D square lattice or a fully connected graph — where they periodically consult a random set of q neighbors to determine their next behavioral state. If the consulted neighbors exhibit a unanimous state of churn, the agent will adhere to that opinion. However, if there is a lack of consensus, the agent's internal state is influenced by a unique internal "burning tree" matrix that represents their cognitive process, where the probability of internal "conviction" spreading across the grid is conditioned by their specific brand loyalty and social susceptibility. This design ensures that agents are not merely stochastic variables, but complex entities with structural resilience, whose final decision to churn occurs only when the combined force of social and external influence successfully percolates through their cognitive grid to form a spanning cluster.
